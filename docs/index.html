Lunar Navigator

When are the Sun and Earth both geometrically above the horizon at a candidate lunar south-polar site?

Geometric center of the Sun and Earth above a smooth spherical tangent plane. Not terrain-aware, not apparent-disk visibility, not power, thermal, or communications assessment. Coordinates provisionally interpreted as planetocentric in MOON_ME_DE421. Hourly UTC samples; no interpolation.

Primary result — 1–14 January 2028

Largest simultaneous-availability contrast in the fixed three-site, three-window set. Simultaneous means both geometric centers are above the smooth horizon at the same hourly sample.

How to Read This Visualization

The chart below shows geometric elevation of the Sun’s center (ochre) and Earth’s center (copper) versus time for the two sites during 1–14 January 2028.

  • Vertical axis: elevation in degrees above the smooth geometric horizon (0°). Positive is above the horizon; negative is below.
  • Horizontal axis: hourly UTC samples from 1 January 2028 00:00 to 14 January 2028 23:00 (336 samples).
  • Ochre line: Sun-center elevation.
  • Copper line: Earth-center elevation.
  • Dashed line at 0°: geometric horizon. A body is counted “above” only when its line is above this mark.
  • Simultaneous: both lines are above 0° at the same sample. That is the quantity compared between the two sites.

What the result shows: during this window, DM2-358 has both bodies above the horizon for nearly the entire period (324/336 samples). SITE07-1223 has both above for a smaller fraction (237/336). The difference is the primary demonstration of the dataset.

Data and method

  • Three published points from Peña-Asensio et al. (provisional MOON_ME_DE421 interpretation).
  • Hourly UTC samples over inclusive 14-day windows; no interpolation.
  • Skyfield + pinned DE421 kernels. Counts are reproducible from the CSV.
  • Simultaneous visibility = both geometric centers above the smooth tangent plane at the same sample.
